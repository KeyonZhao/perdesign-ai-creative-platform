# Argument Reconstruction Method

## Objective

Turn source material into a navigable argument model, not a visual table of contents. A reader who never sees the source should still understand the evidence, reasoning, opportunity, decision, and execution logic.

## Default argument chain

### 1. Demand clarification

Separate:

- explicit request: the form or feature the client asks for;
- latent business objective: differentiation, premium, conversion, reputation, or growth;
- constraints: cost, technology, environment, brand, behavior, or trust;
- reframed research question: what must be proven before accepting the requested form.

Stage output: a problem worth researching, not a preselected answer.

### 2. Problem research

Identify contradictions at several levels:

- category contradiction;
- value perception contradiction;
- current interface/journey contradiction;
- capability versus user understanding;
- short-term appeal versus long-term use;
- failed assumptions and why they fail.

Stage output: the key problem the innovation must solve.

### 3. Audience analysis

Do not stop at demographics. Cover:

- household or organizational context;
- roles and stakeholders;
- jobs to be done;
- anxieties and desired certainty;
- willingness to pay;
- adoption barriers;
- competing desires, such as rich capability versus low complexity.

Stage output: the value users will actually purchase or adopt.

### 4. Scenario research

For each priority scene, include:

- trigger;
- actors;
- current behavior;
- concrete problem;
- why current solutions fail;
- the value that could be proven;
- success signal.

Stage output: situations where the opportunity becomes observable and testable.

### 5. Competitor analysis

Separate:

- direct competitors and common capability baseline;
- their dominant value language;
- current product/interface pattern;
- gaps and overused conventions;
- cross-industry products shaping user expectations;
- what can be borrowed;
- what cannot be copied because of category differences;
- the focal brand's credible right to win.

Stage output: a defensible white space, not merely a feature gap.

### 6. Opportunity synthesis

Use explicit filters:

- user value;
- category relevance;
- brand credibility;
- technical feasibility;
- commercial sustainability;
- explainability and trust;
- portfolio extensibility.

Show the inference chain as separate claims. Include rejected directions and their rejection reasons.

Stage output: a precise product opportunity statement.

### 7. Product definition and strategy

Translate opportunity into:

- product role and definition;
- core value proposition;
- function system;
- information architecture;
- interaction division;
- visual/product language;
- variants or portfolio strategy;
- priority scenarios;
- technical/configuration tiers;
- channel and communication strategy;
- commercial value;
- risk-countermeasure pairs;
- execution summary.

## Parent-child logic

Apply four tests to every parent:

1. Summary test: Can the parent accurately summarize every direct child?
2. Same-dimension test: Are siblings all problems, audiences, scenes, functions, risks, or another single dimension?
3. Information-gain test: Does each child add a distinct claim, fact, example, or action?
4. Necessity test: Would removing the child weaken the reasoning or execution model?

## Writing rules

- Weak: `用户需求`
- Strong: `用户购买的是被照顾的确定感，而不是屏幕本身`
- Weak: `竞品分析`
- Strong: `直接竞品能力趋同，但高端价值表达仍弱`
- Weak: `卧室场景`
- Strong: `卧室需要智能守护，但拒绝持续光线和信息打扰`

Titles should express claims. Notes should retain evidence, examples, limits, and recommended actions.
