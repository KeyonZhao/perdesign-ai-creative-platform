# Hisense Quality Reference

Use this only as a quality and reasoning reference. Do not copy its category-specific conclusions into unrelated projects.

## Input challenge

The client explicitly wanted a screen on an air conditioner. A weak map would start with screen features. The strong map first reframed the request: the real objective was differentiation, premium perception, a new product language, and better communication of high-end value.

## Reconstructed argument

1. Demand clarification: a screen was the requested form, not the proven opportunity.
2. Problem research: air is a high-need but low-visibility medium; premium capabilities were present but hard to perceive.
3. Audience analysis: high-end households wanted certainty that air and family members were being cared for, without complex operation.
4. Scenario research: living-room gatherings, child activity, sleep, humid seasons, and extreme heat made the value testable.
5. Competitor analysis: direct competitors converged on comfort, health, AI, and energy efficiency; cars, refrigerators, and control screens raised information-organization expectations.
6. Opportunity synthesis: the screen should visualize air state, translate parameters, explain AI behavior, and show long-term value. Entertainment, universal control, and decorative animation were rejected.
7. Product definition: define an air-visualization interface, then derive function system, living-room/bedroom variants, configuration tiers, retail demonstration, commercial value, and risk controls.

## Example claim lineage

```text
Air is invisible and multi-factorial
└─ Users feel discomfort but cannot diagnose it
   └─ Existing displays show parameters, not causes or value
      └─ Opportunity: translate air state into life language
         ├─ Function: state visualization
         ├─ Function: AI recommendation
         ├─ Function: airflow visualization
         └─ Trust rule: explain decisions and avoid absolute claims
```

## What made the map strong

- Seven visible stages before the final innovation strategy.
- 288 topics with meaningful depth, not mechanical fragmentation.
- Each stage had an explicit output.
- Features were traceable to problems and scenarios.
- Living-room and bedroom products diverged because their spaces and attention requirements differed.
- Risks were paired with countermeasures.
- Topic details preserved evidence and examples while titles stayed scannable.
